Qian Lou

Bio

Qian Lou is a researcher and professor at the University of Central Florida, serving as Principal Investigator on projects supported by NSF, NIH, and Amazon. His research develops AI infrastructure for secure and private computation, private verification, and privacy-preserving learning and data synthesis. He has developed new cryptographic primitives and compilers that enable new AI applications, and develops AI agents for cryptanalysis and cryptographic programming. His goal is to unlock the value of sensitive and distributed data while expanding AI's capabilities and applications.

He received the NSF CAREER Award in 2026. His work appears in leading AI, security, and systems venues, including NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, IEEE S&P, and DAC. Before joining UCF, he was a Senior Research Scientist at Samsung AI Center in Mountain View. His work has received a Samsung Research Best Paper Award and Best Paper Finalist honors at PACT and ICCAD.

Research

How can AI infrastructure enable secure and private computation, private verification, and private learning and data synthesis?

Driven by this question, our research aims to unlock sensitive data without exposing it, enable verification over private data, and unleash the potential of underused “dark data” and distributed data. Through these advances, we aim to expand AI's capabilities, scale its deployment, and enable new applications.

We pursue this goal through two connected directions: Crypto for AI and AI for Crypto. Crypto for AI pursues native-complexity, general-purpose, and verifiable homomorphic encryption (nuvFHE) primitives and sub-linear zero-knowledge proofs for general matrix multiplication (redZKP), and develops compilers such as CipherFlow. Our compiler dynamically and automatically maps AI computation graphs from frameworks such as PyTorch to secure computation graphs using fully homomorphic encryption (FHE), nuvFHE, secure multiparty computation (MPC), and related techniques. These efforts support secure applications in AI training, inference, verification, and data synthesis. We also combine cryptographic foundations with complementary techniques, including trusted hardware and differential privacy. AI for Crypto develops agents for cryptanalysis and for generating and optimizing cryptographic programs under explicit functional and security checks.

I. Crypto for AI

We co-design cryptographic primitives, compilers, and applications to make private computation and verification practical for AI.

Primitives for private computation and verification

We pursue native-complexity, general-purpose, and verifiable homomorphic encryption through nuvFHE, alongside redZKP for sub-linear zero-knowledge proofs for general matrix multiplication. Our foundational work addresses complementary challenges:

  • Efficient encrypted computation. SHE (NeurIPS 2019) co-designs model representations and encrypted arithmetic to reduce computation costs. [Paper]
  • General-purpose encrypted operations. Universal-BGV (IEEE S&P 2026) connects arithmetic and comparison within one FHE scheme. [Paper] [Code]
  • Verifiable encrypted computation. DataSeal (IEEE S&P 2025) adds checks for the correctness of supported encrypted computations. [Paper]
  • Private verification. zkVC (DAC 2025) accelerates zero-knowledge proofs for matrix computations while protecting the server's private information. [Paper] [Code]

Compilers connecting AI models to secure execution

Our compiler research connects AI computation graphs with secure execution, coordinating model transformations, cryptographic protocols, and hardware choices.

  • CipherFlow (NeurIPS 2026). Plans hybrid secure inference using model dependencies and measured hardware and network costs, reducing redundant planning work while coordinating protocol and placement choices. [Publication]
  • HEMET (ICML 2021) and SAFENet (ICLR 2021). Establish model co-design techniques that reduce protected inference costs while accounting for accuracy. [HEMET paper] [SAFENet paper]

Applications enabled by privacy and verification

We use these foundations to support private learning, inference, verification, and data synthesis. Where appropriate, we combine cryptographic protection with trusted hardware and differential privacy.

  • Private learning. DictPFL (NeurIPS 2025) supports federated learning with encrypted gradients. [Paper]
  • Private inference. SecureRoute (DAC 2026) protects adaptive model selection and inference using MPC. The public preprint is titled SecureRouter. [Paper]
  • Private data synthesis. FHAIM (ICML 2026) combines encrypted computation with differential privacy to protect source data and the release of synthetic data. [Paper]
  • Healthcare AI. Our ongoing NIH R01 develops evidence-grounded collaborative agents for bacterial infection decision support, motivating protected access to sensitive institutional data alongside clinical validation and clinician oversight. [Funding]

II. AI for Crypto

We develop AI agents for cryptanalysis and for generating and optimizing cryptographic software, with functional and security requirements guiding the process.

  • Cryptographic programming. FHE-Coder (ICLR 2026) combines task formalization, relevant documentation, and external checks to generate functional and secure FHE programs. [Paper] [Benchmark & project]
  • Cryptographic optimization (ongoing). We study agents that improve implementations using execution measurements while preserving functional and security requirements.
  • Cryptanalysis (ongoing). We investigate AI-assisted analysis of cryptographic constructions and implementations.

Connecting the two directions. Protected AI applications reveal performance bottlenecks and implementation needs. Our longer-term goal is to use this evidence to guide AI-assisted cryptographic development, with proposed improvements undergoing validation before returning to the secure computing stack.
